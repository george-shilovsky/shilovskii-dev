Privacy policy

This Privacy Policy explains how the Verigate application ("Verigate", "we", "us", "our") handles information when you use it on your iPhone or iPad. We are Georgii Shilovskii, an individual developer registered in Mexico, contactable at support@shilovskii.dev.

If you do not agree with this Privacy Policy, please do not install or use Verigate.

Summary

By default, everything stays on your iPhone. Scan results, guest records, lists, and settings live in the app on your device. There is no account, advertising, tracking, or third-party analytics, and no ID image or raw license number is ever stored. Barcode scanning takes no still image; optional front-ID OCR processes one temporary image on-device and discards it after recognition.

Team events are the one exception, and you choose them. From Verigate 2.0, an organizer can start a team event so that several phones at several doors share guest records. Those records are end-to-end encrypted on the phones with a key that our relay server never has, so the relay stores and forwards them without being able to read them. To make this work, the relay does see a small amount of technical data — random installation IDs, public keys, whether each Staff phone has Premium, and the type, time, and size of each record — and Google Cloud keeps request logs, including IP addresses, for 30 days. Everything an event leaves on the relay is deleted with the event. Details are in section 4.

This policy covers two roles: how we handle data about you (very little, and only for team events), and your own responsibility for the data you collect about your guests when you use Verigate in a business — see section 8.

2. Data we collect about you

If you never use team events, none. Verigate contains no analytics, telemetry, advertising, or crash-reporting code and no third-party SDKs, and we do not collect usage data or advertising identifiers.

If you start or join a team event, our relay receives the technical data listed in section 4. In the categories Apple uses on the App Store, that is:

  • Identifiers — Device ID: a random installation ID that Verigate creates on your device (not a hardware or advertising identifier).
  • Diagnostics — Other diagnostic data: the IP address and User-Agent (app build and iOS version) of each request, kept in Google Cloud request logs for 30 days.
  • Purchases — Purchase history: whether a Staff phone joins with Verigate Premium or without it.

We use this data only to run team events. It is not linked to your name, email, or Apple ID — Verigate has no accounts — and it is never used for tracking or advertising. The guest records themselves are end-to-end encrypted and readable only on the team's phones.

3. Network behaviour

Verigate opens network connections only in these situations:

  • Purchases. Apple's StoreKit framework communicates with the App Store when you start, restore, or refresh a purchase. That traffic is handled by iOS and governed by Apple's Privacy Policy; we receive no payment details.
  • Team events. When you use team events in Settings → Team — start or join one, or take part in one — Verigate talks to our relay over HTTPS and uses Apple's App Attest service to prove that it is a genuine copy of the app (see section 4).
  • Links. The website, support, privacy, and terms links open in your browser only when you tap them.

Verigate makes no other outbound connection. Scanning, verdicts, History, lists, and reports work offline. Transfer Lists produces a Ban/VIP file that you move between devices yourself; it does not go through the relay.

4. Team events

A team event lets an organizer with Verigate Premium run several doors as one event: up to 10 Staff phones scan for the same event and see the same guests, Ban list, and VIP list, and the organizer gets one report at the end. Taking part is always a choice — the organizer starts the event, and each Staff phone joins by scanning the organizer's code and being added by the organizer. A plain-language walkthrough is on How Team events work.

End-to-end encryption

The organizer's phone creates a secret key for each event and hands it only to the phones the organizer adds, protected by each phone's own keys. Every guest record, decision, list change, and event detail is encrypted on the phone that makes it and decrypted only on the team's phones. Our relay never has the event key, so it stores and forwards ciphertext it cannot read.

Inside the team, everyone on the event can read its records. The organizer decides who joins and sets each phone's name and door. A Staff phone can leave the team on its own at any time; to take every phone off the team, the organizer ends the event and deletes it from the relay.

Where it runs

The relay runs on Google Cloud (Cloud Run and Firestore) in the us-central1 region in the United States. Like any web service, Google Cloud logs each request the relay receives — the IP address, User-Agent, requested path (which contains the event's random ID), time, status, and size. These logs are kept for 30 days and then deleted. The relay's own log lines record only which operation ran and its result, never IDs or content. Access to the relay's database and logs is limited to the developer's Google Cloud account.

App Attest

Before a device can use the relay, Verigate asks Apple's App Attest service to certify a key created in the device's Secure Enclave. The relay checks Apple's certificate itself and does not send your data to Apple. App Attest is provided by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy. Team events are available only on devices that support it.

How long the relay keeps an event

  • Delete from relay. After ending an event, the organizer can delete it from the relay; the relay erases it right away.
  • Ended but not deleted. The relay keeps the encrypted records only while the phones finish sending — late records are accepted for 72 hours after End, and nothing is kept longer than 14 days after End — and then deletes them in its routine clean-up.
  • Never ended. An event that its organizer never ends is deleted automatically once it is 30 days old, the same way as Delete from relay.

The routine clean-up runs while the relay is in use, so a deletion may happen some time after these deadlines rather than at the exact minute. After an event is deleted, the relay keeps for 90 days only a small marker under the event's random ID recording that it was deleted and how it ended — with no installation or member IDs, keys, records, or content — so the same event ID cannot be reused by mistake. Short-lived counters that limit how often a device can call the relay are keyed by a one-way hash of the installation ID and expire on their own.

Leaving, ending, and what stays on the phones

  • A Staff phone can leave a team event at any time with Settings → Team → Leave team event. Leaving works offline: the guest records it received from the team are removed from that phone (on a phone without Premium, its own scans from the event too), and records it has not sent yet never reach the team.
  • Staff phones remove the guest records they received from the team the next time they connect after the event is deleted from the relay (by the organizer's Delete from relay or by the relay's routine clean-up described above) or after its 72-hour window for late records closes, whichever comes first. A Staff phone without Premium also removes its own scans from that event; a Staff phone with Premium keeps its own scans. Between End and that point, Staff phones keep the event's records only to finish sending late ones.
  • The organizer keeps the event's records and report on their phone, like any other local data.
  • Data that has already reached another phone stays on that phone under its owner's control until removed as described above; neither we nor the organizer can erase it remotely.

5. How identity recognition works (and why it's private)

To recognise a returning, VIP, or banned document, Verigate normalises the license number, combines it with a validated issuer identifier — or, conservatively, its country and jurisdiction — and computes a SHA-256 digest. It stores the digest, never the raw number. The digest is one-way and is derived from the document number, not from a face or fingerprint, so it is not biometric data. In a team event, the digest travels inside the encrypted records so that other team phones recognise the same document; the relay cannot read it.

A person added manually or from CSV may initially have only a name and date of birth. If a later scan suggests a match, the operator must decide explicitly whether it is the same person; Verigate never merges people automatically.

6. Permissions

Verigate requests only the Camera, for PDF417 barcode scanning, optional front-ID OCR in Manual Entry, and scanning the organizer's code when joining a team event. Barcode and code scanning take no still image. If you choose OCR, one temporary image is held in memory, processed entirely on-device, and discarded after recognition. Verigate does not request Photos, Location, Microphone, Contacts, Local Network, or any other permission.

7. Retention and deletion

  • You control local retention: Settings → Privacy → Auto-delete logs (Never / 30 days / 90 days).
  • Clear scan history deletes checks and unflagged people; your Ban/VIP lists and operational catalog stay. Clear all data deletes entries, people, operational context, and legacy compatibility Profiles.
  • Team event data on the relay is deleted as described in section 4: right away with Delete from relay, no later than 14 days after End, or once the event is 30 days old if it is never ended. Google Cloud request logs are deleted after 30 days.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all local data from that device.

8. Your guests' data — the operator's responsibility

If you use Verigate in a business (a bar, club, shop, or event), you decide to scan other people's identity documents and you control the resulting records. In doing so you may be a "data controller" (or equivalent) under the laws of your jurisdiction — for example US state privacy and ID-scanning statutes, Illinois BIPA and similar biometric laws, Canadian PIPEDA, or the EU/UK GDPR.

You are responsible for scanning lawfully, for any notices or consent your local law requires, for retention limits, and for how you use flags and notes. If you organize a team event, you also decide which phones receive your guests' records, and you are responsible for sharing them with the people you add. We provide privacy-preserving tooling: no stored ID image, no raw number, and end-to-end encryption for team events. Outside team events we have no access to the data you collect about your guests; for team events our relay only stores and forwards it in encrypted form that we cannot read. Requests from your guests about their data should be directed to you.

Note that the people an operator scans may themselves be under the age threshold — detecting that is the point of the check. The operator is responsible for handling any such data — including minors' data — lawfully under applicable law (see section 8).

11. Your privacy rights

Most of your data never reaches us, so the rights typically granted under the GDPR, CCPA, and similar privacy laws — access, correction, deletion, and export — are exercised by you, on the device:

  • Access. Every scan is visible in History; every flagged guest in the Ban/VIP lists; team event reports in Settings → Team.
  • Correction. Names, nicknames, notes, and flags are editable in-app. Admit/Deny corrections create a visible audit revision rather than silently replacing the original decision.
  • Deletion. Clear scan history, Clear all data, or delete the app. For team events: Leave team event on a Staff phone, and End then Delete from relay on the organizer's phone.
  • Portability. Premium CSV/PDF export, the event report, and the device-to-device list transfer.
  • Withdrawal of consent. Stop using team events, or delete the app.

The technical data the relay holds for team events is not linked to any name, email address, or account, so we cannot look it up by person; it is deleted on the schedule in section 4. If you have questions about your rights, contact us at support@shilovskii.dev and we will help you exercise them.

12. Cookies and tracking technologies

Verigate is a native iOS application. It does not use cookies, web beacons, pixels, or any other web-tracking technology, and it does not track you across apps or websites. Do-Not-Track signals are therefore not applicable — there is nothing for us to track.
